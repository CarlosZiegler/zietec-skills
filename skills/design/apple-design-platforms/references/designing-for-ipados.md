# Designing for iPadOS

- Use the display to expose meaningful content and related work together. Reduce unnecessary full-screen transitions and modal interruptions.
- Adapt density to viewing distance and input precision rather than simply enlarging an iPhone layout.
- Support touch, keyboard, pointer, Pencil, and combinations of inputs where relevant. An attached accessory should enhance the same task.
- Plan for multitasking, window changes, orientations, large text, and different appearances. Preserve selection and working context during adaptation.
- Include useful inter-app operations such as drag and drop when the content supports them.

Consult current windowing and navigation guidance for the target OS. A large display does not imply that the app always has a wide window, and pointer support does not remove the need for comfortable touch interaction.

Source: [Apple HIG — Designing for iPadOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ipados). Checked 2026-09-08.
