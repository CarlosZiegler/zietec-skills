# Designing for iOS

- Prioritize the main task and content on a handheld display. Keep secondary actions discoverable without filling the screen with controls.
- Account for one- and two-handed use. Important repeated interactions should be comfortable to reach; preserve familiar swipe navigation and row actions.
- Support both brief visits and longer sessions. Treat app switching and resumption as normal parts of use.
- Adapt to orientation, appearance, and Dynamic Type without breaking the information hierarchy or losing context.
- Use device capabilities to reduce effort only when they serve the task and the person grants the relevant permission.
- Consider widgets, shortcuts, Spotlight, sharing, and Home Screen quick actions when they provide a useful entry point.

For actual screens, follow the current component guidance as well as these platform priorities. This overview does not define every control’s placement, dimensions, or availability.

Source: [Apple HIG — Designing for iOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ios). Checked 2026-09-08.
