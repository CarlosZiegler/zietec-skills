# Design principles

Use the eight principles as lenses for resolving a specific design tradeoff:

- Purpose: prioritize the task that makes the product worth using; explain how proposed features serve it.
- Agency: preserve freedom to explore, keep outcomes understandable, and make recovery inexpensive.
- Responsibility: earn trust through transparent behavior and careful handling of people’s information.
- Familiarity: build on established concepts and keep visual and interaction meanings consistent.
- Flexibility: accommodate diverse people, inputs, devices, and contexts without losing orientation.
- Simplicity: remove unnecessary burden while retaining the controls and information the task needs.
- Craft: refine behavior, language, visuals, sound, and performance through realistic use and iteration.
- Delight: choose an appropriate emotional character and express it through useful, considered interactions.

Apply only the lenses relevant to the decision. Explain competing benefits instead of treating these principles as a numerical score. The current source introduces this eight-principle set in its June 2026 update; older summaries may use different organizing terms.

Source: [Apple HIG — Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles). Checked 2026-09-08.
