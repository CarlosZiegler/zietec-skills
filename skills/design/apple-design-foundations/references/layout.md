# Layout

- Establish hierarchy through grouping, alignment, position, and space. Keep essential information and controls easy to locate.
- Let content extend into available space while respecting safe areas for interactive elements and system features.
- Distinguish navigation and controls from the content they operate on. Use progressive disclosure where it improves comprehension.
- Adapt continuously to window size, text size, localization, and orientation while maintaining recognizable relationships.
- On iOS, aim to support both orientations and consult the current guidance before defaulting to full-width buttons. On iPad, preserve a useful wider layout as long as it remains workable while resizing. On iPhone Duo, adapt through size classes rather than per-pose layouts, and keep interactive content out of reserved regions such as the fold and the vertical bar rail ([Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)).

Use current platform layout guides and inspect actual content at narrow and intermediate widths. Device dimensions and size-class tables are reference data to verify, not universal constants to embed in every design.

Source: [Apple HIG — Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout). Checked 2026-09-08.
